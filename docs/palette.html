The drift

A day has a temperature

Timestamps take their tint from the hour they name — warm at dawn and dusk, colourless at noon, cool after midnight. Hues come off the Planckian locus for the warm half and the CIE daylight locus for the cool half; they cross at near-zero chroma around 6500 K, so midday is genuinely neutral. Interpolation runs in OKLab a/b rather than hue angle, so mid-morning passes through true grey instead of detouring through green.

The whole ramp lives at chroma 0.002–0.038 while every colour that means something lives at 0.075–0.185. That gap is what keeps this a thermometer instead of a rainbow — no hour can be mistaken for a habit state. And lightness is pinned at every hour, so the drift costs nothing: the floor across all 24 is 4.62:1 light, 6.52:1 dark.

The decision the rest follows from

Kept and broken differ mostly in brightness

The first draft put teal at L 0.54 and madder at L 0.56 — near-identical lightness, separated by hue alone. Measured on the pair the glyphs actually render, it kept just 14% of its separation under protanopia: worse than plain green/red. Dichromacy compresses hue and leaves luminance almost untouched, so a hue-only pair has nothing left to show. Opening a lightness gap of ΔL 0.28 — the same two hues — is what fixed it.

Protanopia in the light theme, at 48%, is the weakest cell and is stated rather than buried — protanopes see red as much darker than it is, which pulls a pale madder back down toward the teal it is meant to separate from. It is also why the glyphs are not decoration: kept is a filled ring, broken is a ring struck through, pending is a dashed ring, passive is a diamond. Those four silhouettes are distinguishable with the colour channel removed entirely, and the word is printed beside every one of them anyway.

How this was arrived at

Four decisions

Blue leads, violet serves

Cyanotype takes the identity at hue 248 and the neutrals follow it, so the whole interface reads at one temperature. Iris stays in the palette but drops to chroma 0.075 as the machine voice — ring data, screen time, anything that arrived without being typed.

Lightness carries the verdict

Roughly one man in twelve has a red–green deficiency, and the most-read signal in this app is a binary habit state. Hue cannot be trusted with it. Teal and madder stay, but 0.31 of lightness now sits between them — which is what survives when hue does not.

Two registers, split by chroma

Time lives at chroma 0.002–0.038, meaning lives at 0.075–0.185. The gap is wide enough that an hour can never be misread as a state. It lets the interface breathe with the day without the day ever competing with the data.

Failure recedes

A broken habit gets a pale wash and a struck ring, not a red alarm. This is a private log kept by one person about their own life; the brightest thing on any screen should be something they actually did.

Colours computed in OKLCH with Ottosson's matrices and gamut-fitted into sRGB by bisection on chroma, so no coordinate here sits outside sRGB. Contrast is WCAG 2.1 relative luminance against each theme's own ground. Simulations use the Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999) LMS matrices in linear RGB — the same matrices drive the live filters above and the table numbers, so the swatches you toggle and the figures agree. Tritanopia is the least reliable of the three: Viénot's single-plane construction is an approximation there, and the condition affects on the order of 1 in 10,000 people rather than 1 in 12.
